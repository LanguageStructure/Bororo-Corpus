How to use CorBo

CorBo can be used as a reader of Bororo texts, as a parallel corpus and as an environment for linguistic exploration. Not every unit has the same degree of revision or annotation; the tools show the layers that exist.

1. Read the texts

Open Texts to choose a collection or narrative. In parallel readers the Bororo text appears with its Portuguese translation when available. Some collections also keep sections, original numbering, variants, collation status or editorial notes.

2. Click on Bororo words

In the readers, clicking a Bororo word opens a side panel without leaving the text. It shows, when available: part of speech and lemma, morphological features in plain words, the annotated morpheme segmentation (e.g. e = ako = re), the tokenizer analysis, Bororo Dictionary entries, examples from the corpus with translation, the distribution across collections, and links to all occurrences, a dependency tree and the dictionary. Use ← and → to move to the previous or next word, and Esc to close.

3. Choose the kind of search

Concordance (Search) finds occurrences in the texts and shows each result inside its sentence (KWIC), with filters by collection and editorial status. Word search (Word search) returns only word forms, with their frequency: by form, by lemma (as annotated in CoNLL-U, e.g. ako) or by morpheme (only explicitly annotated segmentations, e.g. iagu). Rule of thumb: for sentences use Concordance; for lists of words use Word search.

4. Explore the linguistic analysis

Form analysis, Morphemes, Linguistic relations and the CoNLL-U viewer show the annotation that exists. The viewer only displays sentences present in the canonical CoNLL-U file and does not project trees onto other texts.

5. Source, revision, collation and annotation

CorBo distinguishes the documentary source, editorial revision, collation between witnesses and linguistic annotation; the source spelling is never silently replaced by a normalisation. In collated witnesses, unique means no textual equivalent was found in the public corpus; parallel_formulaic marks a narrative or formulaic parallel without strict documentary equivalence; confirmed identifies a correspondence already represented (so it is not published again); unresolved stays out of the public index until human review.
