About the project

About CorBo

CorBo is a digital corpus of the Bororo (Boe) language, designed as infrastructure for language documentation, linguistic research, computational annotation and the development of resources for the language.

The corpus

The project brings together materials of different kinds within a common architecture: documentary texts, narratives and other materials from fieldwork and archives, texts with translation, biblical materials, and data prepared for linguistic and computational analysis. Collections are not treated as if they all had the same editorial status, and each unit keeps the link between the documentary source and later stages of work.

CorBo therefore separates explicitly the documentary source, the revised form, collation between witnesses, optional normalisation layers and linguistic annotation. An editorial correction never silently replaces the source. This makes the corpus usable both for documentary research and for grammatical, quantitative and computational studies.

Revision and correction

Preparing the corpus involves continuous manual revision. Fabrício Ferraz Gerardi checks transcriptions and Bororo forms, makes editorial corrections where needed, aligns texts with translations and organises the units. Doubtful readings, gaps, numbering differences and source particularities are preserved or recorded rather than automatically regularised. A unit marked as revised has passed an editorial stage of the project; this does not mean that its documentation is closed, and later checks may produce new versions.

CoNLL-U and Universal Dependencies

Linguistic annotation is kept in CoNLL-U: sentence segmentation, forms and lemmas, parts of speech, morphological features and dependency relations, following Universal Dependencies where applicable to Bororo. All manual annotation and CoNLL-U corrections are by Fabrício Ferraz Gerardi; automatically filled fields are marked (Auto=) and kept apart in the gold/silver layers. The site tools show the existing analysis; they do not create analyses for unannotated data.

Fieldwork, archives and future versions

The corpus includes, and will keep incorporating, materials from fieldwork, archives and language documentation, extending it beyond previously published material and representing different genres, speakers and contexts. Incorporation is gradual: transcription, checking, translation, revision and annotation may happen at different times. Version numbers record this progress and make it possible to cite a specific state of the corpus.

People, partners and communities

Project, organisation, fieldwork, editorial revision and linguistic annotation: Fabrício Ferraz Gerardi. Partners: Mariel Kujibo Ekureu, Fernando Kudoro and the Salesian Mission; Salesian missionaries who lived in Meruri are the authors of Boe Ero (Vida Bororo) and other materials. Communities: Meruri village (Meruri Indigenous Land), Tadarimana and Córrego Grande. The project is part of the Boe eno moto initiative.
